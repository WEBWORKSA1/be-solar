The sizing formula

System size in kW = annual kWh you want to cover ÷ (peak sun hours × 365 × 0.8). The 0.8 factor covers real-world losses. Panel count assumes 430-watt modules, the common size for residential panels today; each needs roughly 2 square metres of usable roof including spacing.

Fire codes in many regions require clear walkways along ridges and edges, so the roof area you need is usually 15 to 25% more than the panel footprint. South-facing roofs (north-facing in the southern hemisphere) give the most energy; east and west roofs typically produce 10 to 20% less but can suit time-of-use tariffs better.

When to size below 100%

If your utility credits exported energy at a low rate, size for the energy you use during daylight, often 60 to 80% of total usage, and consider a battery for the rest.

Frequently asked

How many solar panels does an average home need?

A home using about 10,000 kWh a year in a location with 4.5 peak sun hours needs a 7 to 8 kW system, or about 17 to 19 panels at 430 W.

Can I add panels later?

Yes, but adding to an existing system can mean a second inverter and a new permit. It is usually cheaper to size for planned changes, such as an EV or heat pump, up front.
