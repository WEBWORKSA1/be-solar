How this calculator works

It converts your monthly bill into annual electricity use by dividing by your price per kWh. It then sizes a system to cover the share you choose, using your daily peak sun hours and an 80% performance ratio that accounts for inverter losses, heat, wiring and dirt.

Savings in each year equal the energy the system produces (capped at your own usage, so exports are not over-counted) multiplied by an electricity price that rises by the rate you set. Panel output falls by 0.5% a year, in line with typical tier-one warranties. Payback is the point where cumulative savings exceed the price after incentives.

What it leaves out on purpose

  • Export payments. Many utilities now credit exported power below the retail price. If yours pays full retail net metering, your real savings will be higher.
  • Financing costs. Loan interest lengthens payback; use the payback and ROI calculator to compare cash with a loan.
  • Maintenance and inverter replacement, typically a modest cost over 25 years for string inverters.
Make it precise: replace the default installed cost with the price per watt from a real quote, and the electricity price with the all-in rate from your bill (total charges divided by kWh used).

Frequently asked

What is a good payback period for solar?

Under 10 years is strong in most markets; 6 to 8 years is typical where electricity is expensive. Panels usually carry 25-year performance warranties, so anything well inside that window means decades of profit.

What are peak sun hours?

The number of hours per day the sun would need to shine at 1,000 watts per square metre to deliver your location's daily solar energy. It is not the same as daylight hours.

Why is my estimate different from an installer's?

Installers use your exact roof pitch, direction, shading and utility tariff. Use this result as a sanity check: if a quote's production or price is far off, ask why.
