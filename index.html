How going solar actually works

Four decisions, in this order. Most regret comes from doing them backwards.

  1. Check the fit

    Roof direction, shade, roof age and your local electricity price decide whether solar pays. Our calculators answer this in a minute.

  2. Size it right

    Match the system to your real usage, not the biggest array a roof can hold. Oversizing wastes money where export rates are low.

  3. Compare three quotes

    Prices for identical systems often differ by a third or more. Compare cost per watt, equipment and warranty, not monthly payment.

  4. Claim every incentive

    Subsidies, export payments and tax treatment differ by country and change often. Our incentive pages track the current rules.

Quick answers

Is solar still worth it in 2026?

In most places with average or higher electricity prices and a sunny, unshaded roof, a fairly priced system still pays back well within its 25-year warranty. Run the savings calculator with your own bill to see your break-even year.

How much does a home solar system cost?

Installed prices vary by country. In the US most quotes land between about $2.50 and $3.50 per watt before incentives, so a 7 kW system is roughly $18,000 to $25,000. The calculators let you change the cost per watt to match quotes you receive.

Do I need a battery?

Not to save money in most grid-connected homes. A battery is worth it when your utility pays little for exported power, charges peak-time rates, or when outages are frequent. Use the battery calculator to size one.
