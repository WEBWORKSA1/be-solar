Solar incentives in Canada in 2026: what's still available

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

Federal grants and loans have closed. Here's how provincial, utility and municipal programs fill the gap.

For several years, Canadian homeowners could combine a federal Greener Homes Grant and an interest-free loan to pay for solar. Both have closed to new applicants, so solar in Canada now depends on provincial, utility and municipal programs and on local electricity prices.

Federal programs

  • Canada Greener Homes Grant: closed in all provinces. It previously paid up to $5,000 for eligible retrofits including solar.
  • Canada Greener Homes Loan: the interest-free loan is fully committed and no longer approving new applications.
  • Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program: announced in 2026 to provide no-cost retrofits to low- and median-income households through provinces. Its focus is heat pumps and building envelope upgrades rather than solar.

Provincial and utility programs

Programs differ greatly between provinces and change often. Common types:

  • Utility rebates per watt installed or for batteries.
  • Low-interest municipal financing repaid through property taxes (often called PACE or LIC programs).
  • Net metering or net billing rules set by each provincial utility or regulator.
  • Sales tax exemptions in some provinces.

Start with your provincial energy ministry and your utility's website. Note that in provinces with low electricity rates, such as Quebec and Manitoba, solar payback is longer.

Do the numbers still work?

Solar works best in provinces with higher electricity prices and good sun, such as parts of Alberta, Saskatchewan, Ontario and Nova Scotia. Canadian winters lower output, but long summer days and cold temperatures (which raise panel efficiency) partly compensate. Snow usually slides off tilted panels.

Model your home in the savings calculator with Canada selected, then enter any provincial rebate in the "Other rebates" field.

Tips for Canadian buyers

  • Ask installers for production estimates that include snow losses.
  • Confirm your utility's net metering rules and how winter credits roll over.
  • Check whether your municipality offers property-tax financing.

Frequently asked

Is there a federal solar tax credit in Canada?

No federal income tax credit exists for residential solar. Businesses can access capital cost allowance treatment for clean energy equipment.

Can I still get the Greener Homes Grant?

No. It has closed to new applications nationwide.
