How solar panels work, from photon to power socket

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

A clear walkthrough of solar cells, inverters, meters and the grid, without the jargon.

A home solar system has four working parts: panels that turn light into direct current, an inverter that turns it into household alternating current, a meter that records what flows to and from the grid, and the wiring and protection devices that keep it safe.

Inside a solar cell

Most panels use crystalline silicon cells. Each cell is a thin wafer treated so one side has extra electrons and the other has spaces for them. Where those layers meet, an electric field forms. When light strikes the cell, its energy knocks electrons loose; the field pushes them one way, creating a current. This is the photovoltaic effect.

A single cell produces about half a volt. Panels wire 54 to 72 cells (or twice as many half-cut cells) in series to reach useful voltages. A modern residential panel is rated around 400 to 450 watts under standard test conditions.

Efficiency and why it matters less than you think

Efficiency is the share of sunlight hitting a panel that becomes electricity. Mainstream panels in 2026 convert roughly 20 to 23%. Higher efficiency means more watts per square metre, which matters on a small roof but barely matters if you have space. Price per watt and warranty usually matter more.

Heat lowers output. The temperature coefficient (around −0.3% per °C for good panels) tells you how much power drops on hot days.

Inverters

  • String inverter: one box converts power from a series of panels. Cheapest, but the weakest panel can limit the string.
  • Optimisers: small units on each panel tune its output before a central inverter. Good for partial shade.
  • Microinverters: one inverter per panel; each works independently and is monitored individually.
  • Hybrid inverter: also manages a battery.

Connection to the grid

Inverter output feeds your main electrical panel. Your home uses solar power first; the surplus flows out to the grid and is recorded by the meter. At night you draw from the grid as normal.

Grid-tied inverters shut down automatically during a power cut to protect line workers. That's why panels alone don't keep the lights on in an outage. Backup requires a battery or a specific inverter with an isolated backup mode.

Monitoring

Most systems include an app showing production in real time. Check it monthly. A sudden drop compared with the same month last year can signal a tripped breaker, a failed inverter or heavy soiling.

Frequently asked

Do solar panels need direct sunlight?

They produce most in direct sun but also generate under diffuse light on overcast days, just less.

What's the difference between kW and kWh?

kW measures power, the rate of flow at a moment. kWh measures energy, power over time. A 7 kW system making full power for one hour produces 7 kWh.
