Is solar worth it in 2026? A five-factor test

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

Five numbers decide whether rooftop solar pays for your home. Here's how to check each one in minutes.

Solar is neither a sure win nor a scam. It is an investment whose return depends on five things you can check before a single installer visits. If four of the five look good, solar is very likely to pay off within its warranty. If two or fewer do, put the money somewhere else.

1. What you pay for electricity

This is the biggest lever. Every kilowatt-hour your panels make replaces one you would have bought, so the higher your rate, the faster solar pays back. Find your all-in rate by dividing the total on a recent bill (including delivery and fixed charges that vary with use) by the kWh you used.

  • Strong: above about $0.20 per kWh in the US and Canada, 24p in the UK, A$0.30 in Australia, or ₹7 in India.
  • Marginal: rates well below these, such as parts of the US Northwest or Quebec, where hydro power keeps prices low.

2. How much sun your roof sees

Installers measure this in peak sun hours: the daily solar energy your location receives, expressed as hours of full-strength sunshine. Phoenix sees around 6, London around 2.7, Delhi around 5. A south-facing roof (north-facing south of the equator) with a pitch of 15 to 40 degrees captures most of it. East and west roofs typically lose 10 to 20%.

Shade matters more than most people think. A tree that shades part of an array at midday can cut its output disproportionately unless the system uses microinverters or optimisers. If your roof is shaded for more than a few hours a day, get a professional shade report.

3. What it costs to install

Compare quotes in price per watt: the total price divided by the system's size in watts. A 7 kW system quoted at $19,600 is $2.80 per watt. National averages give you a benchmark. In the US, marketplace data puts the typical figure in the mid-$2 range per watt before incentives, while all-installs surveys report higher medians. Quotes well above your local norm deserve hard questions.

4. Which incentives you can claim

Incentives can cut 10 to 50% off the price, and they change often. In 2026 the picture looks like this:

  • United States: the 30% federal credit for homeowner-owned systems (Section 25D) ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Leases and power purchase agreements can still benefit from a commercial credit claimed by the provider, within deadlines. State, utility and local programs remain. See our US tax credit guide.
  • India: the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy pays up to ₹78,000 for a 3 kW home system in most states.
  • UK: 0% VAT on installed panels until 31 March 2027, plus export payments under the Smart Export Guarantee.
  • Australia: small-scale technology certificates reduce the upfront price, falling each year until 2030, plus a federal battery discount.
  • Canada: federal homeowner grants and loans for solar have closed; provincial and utility programs vary.

5. How you use and export power

Where utilities credit exported power at the full retail rate (classic net metering), every kWh is worth the same whether you use it or export it. Many utilities have moved to paying less for exports. Then the share of solar power you use yourself, in daylight hours, matters a great deal. Households home during the day, with an EV that charges at midday or a heat pump, get more value from each panel.

Putting it together

Run your numbers through the savings calculator. As a rule of thumb:

  • Payback under 8 years: strong. Get quotes now.
  • 8 to 12 years: reasonable, especially if electricity prices are rising or you plan to stay put.
  • Over 12 years: think twice, check whether a battery, a smaller system, community solar or efficiency upgrades would deliver more per dollar.
Also weigh the roof. If it will need replacing within about 10 years, replace it first. Removing and reinstalling panels later commonly costs several thousand dollars.

Reasons beyond payback

Some owners value price certainty, lower emissions or backup power during outages enough to accept a longer payback. That's legitimate, as long as you decide with clear numbers rather than a salesperson's monthly-payment pitch.

Frequently asked

Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes, at reduced output, typically 10 to 25% of their rating under heavy cloud. Annual sun hours already account for cloudy weather, which is why Germany and the UK have large solar fleets.

How long do panels last?

Most quality panels carry a 25-year performance warranty guaranteeing roughly 80 to 90% of original output, and many keep producing for 30 years or more. Inverters typically need replacement once in that period unless they are microinverters with long warranties.
