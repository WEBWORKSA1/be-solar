How much do solar panels cost? Prices by country and system size

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

Typical installed solar prices in the US, Canada, UK, Australia and India, and what moves a quote up or down.

The fairest way to talk about solar prices is cost per watt: the full installed price divided by the system's DC capacity. It lets you compare a 6 kW quote with an 8 kW quote, and your quote with national data.

Why prices differ so much between countries

Hardware costs roughly the same worldwide. The difference is "soft costs": permits, inspections, utility interconnection, sales and marketing, and labour. Australia's streamlined permitting and high-volume installers make it one of the cheapest markets in the world. US prices carry heavy customer-acquisition costs, often thousands of dollars per sale, plus local permitting that varies by town.

What pushes a quote up or down

  • Roof complexity. Multiple roof faces, steep pitches, tile or slate add labour.
  • Equipment tier. Premium panels and microinverters cost more but may produce more on shaded or complex roofs.
  • Electrical upgrades. An older main panel may need replacing, often adding $1,500 to $4,000 in North America.
  • Batteries. Storage typically adds the price of a second system; quote it separately so you can compare.
  • Financing. Loans with "dealer fees" can inflate the cash price by 15 to 30%. Always ask for the cash price.
  • Company type. Local installers often beat national brands on price; national brands may offer stronger long-term service. Compare warranties, not logos.

Price by system size

Larger systems cost less per watt because fixed costs (permits, design, the inverter's base cost, travel) spread over more panels. In the US, a 4 kW system may come in well above the national per-watt average while a 12 kW system comes in below it. Use the system size calculator to find the size you actually need before comparing.

Costs people forget

  • Inverter replacement after 10 to 15 years for string inverters (often $1,500–$3,000).
  • Removal and reinstallation if you reroof.
  • Monitoring subscriptions, which most systems no longer need.
  • Higher home insurance premiums in some markets; check with your insurer.
Quick test: divide any quote's total by its watts. If it is more than about 20% above your local benchmark, ask the installer to explain line by line, then get another quote.

Frequently asked

Is it cheaper to buy panels and install them myself?

Hardware is often less than half of the price, so DIY can save a lot, but most utilities require a licensed electrician to connect, and permits and warranties can be harder to obtain. Plug-in balcony kits are a DIY-friendly option where allowed.

Will solar prices drop if I wait?

Hardware prices have fallen steeply over the past decade, but soft costs fall slowly and incentives in several countries are shrinking on fixed schedules. Each year you wait also costs a year of savings.
