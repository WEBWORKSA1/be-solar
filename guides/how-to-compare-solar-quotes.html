How to compare solar quotes: a 12-point checklist

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

Line up quotes on price per watt, production, equipment and warranties, and spot the tricks that make a bad deal look good.

Getting three quotes is the single most reliable way to save money on solar. The spread between the cheapest and most expensive bid for the same home is often a third or more. But quotes are formatted to make comparison hard. Put each one into the same twelve boxes.

Price

  1. Cash price before incentives. Ask for it even if you plan to finance. It exposes hidden loan fees.
  2. Price per watt. Divide the cash price by the DC system size in watts.
  3. Incentives assumed. Check every incentive the quote subtracts actually applies to you. In the US, any quote still subtracting the 30% homeowner tax credit for a system installed in 2026 is out of date.

Production

  1. Estimated annual kWh. The number that drives savings. Run it past an independent model such as NREL's PVWatts, or our sizing calculator. An estimate more than about 10% above independent models is a red flag.
  2. Cost per kWh produced over 25 years. Divide the net price by 25 years of estimated production. This is the truest comparison between quotes of different sizes.
  3. Shade analysis. Ask how shading was measured: a site visit, drone or satellite.

Equipment

  1. Panel make, model and wattage. Look up the datasheet: efficiency, temperature coefficient and degradation rate.
  2. Inverter type. String, string with optimisers, or microinverters. Microinverters and optimisers help on shaded or multi-face roofs.
  3. Battery, if any. Usable kWh, continuous kW, and whether it backs up the whole home or selected circuits.

Warranties and the company

  1. Three warranties: product (panels, typically 25 years), performance (output guarantee), and workmanship (installer's labour and roof penetrations: 10 years is good, 25 is excellent).
  2. Who stands behind them. If the installer closes, who honours the workmanship warranty? Some offer third-party insured warranties.
  3. Licence and certification. Check the local electrical licence plus relevant certification: NABCEP in North America, MCS in the UK, SAA accreditation in Australia, MNRE-empanelled vendors for India's subsidy.

Common tricks

  • Monthly payment framing. "Pay $150 instead of your $200 bill" hides a 25-year total. Compare totals.
  • Inflated escalation. Projections assuming 5–6% annual electricity price rises make every system look great. Re-run them at 2–3%.
  • Escalator leases. A lease that rises 2.9% a year can outpace your utility.
  • Pressure. "This price expires today" is a sales tactic, not a market fact.

Frequently asked

Should I always pick the cheapest quote?

No. Pick the lowest cost per kWh over 25 years from an installer with solid warranties and reviews. A slightly higher price for much better workmanship cover is often worth it.

How long are quotes valid?

Usually 14 to 30 days, because equipment prices and incentives change. A reasonable installer will extend a deadline while you compare.
