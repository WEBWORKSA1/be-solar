Home solar batteries: are they worth it, and what size?

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

When storage pays, when it's just insurance, and how to size a battery for backup or savings.

Batteries are now attached to a large and growing share of new residential solar systems. US national lab data show storage paired with more than a third of 2025 installs. Popularity doesn't make them right for every home. Decide by which job you need the battery to do.

Three jobs a battery can do

  1. Backup power. Keep essentials running during outages. Value depends on how often your power fails and what an outage costs you.
  2. Self-consumption. Store daytime solar for the evening instead of exporting it cheaply. Pays where export rates are low.
  3. Rate arbitrage and grid services. Charge when power is cheap, discharge at peak, or join a virtual power plant that pays for access to your battery.

When batteries pay for themselves

Storage has the best financial case where export rates are far below import rates, where time-of-use tariffs have a large peak-to-off-peak gap, or where a subsidy covers a big share of the cost. Australia's federal Cheaper Home Batteries program, for instance, has given roughly a 30% upfront discount through certificates since July 2025, with the discount tapering for larger batteries and falling over time.

Where full net metering is available, a battery rarely pays back on savings alone: the grid already "stores" your surplus at full value.

Sizing

  • Capacity (kWh) is how much energy it holds. For evening self-consumption, size near your evening and overnight use, often 8 to 15 kWh.
  • Power (kW) is how much it can deliver at once. Starting a well pump or air conditioner needs a high peak rating.
  • Usable capacity matters more than nominal. Lithium iron phosphate batteries usually allow 90–100% to be used.

Try the battery backup calculator to size for your loads.

Chemistry and lifespan

Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) dominates new home batteries thanks to safety and long cycle life, often warranted for 10 years and thousands of cycles with at least 60 to 70% capacity retained. Check the warranty's throughput limit (total MWh) as well as years.

Installation notes

  • Batteries must be installed to fire codes, often in garages or outside walls rather than living spaces.
  • Whole-home backup may need an automatic transfer switch and a main panel upgrade; critical-loads backup is cheaper.
  • Adding a battery to existing solar is possible. AC-coupled batteries work with almost any inverter.
If outages are rare and your export credit is fair, skip the battery for now and leave space and wiring to add one later.

Frequently asked

How long will a battery power my home?

Divide usable kWh by your hourly load. A 13.5 kWh battery running 1 kW of essentials lasts about 13 hours, longer if solar recharges it during the day.

Can I go completely off-grid?

Possible, but it needs far more storage and panels to cover winter, often plus a generator. For most connected homes, staying grid-tied is much cheaper.
