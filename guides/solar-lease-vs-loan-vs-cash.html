Solar lease vs loan vs cash: which way to pay?

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

How each payment route changes your savings, who keeps the incentives, and what happens when you sell the house.

How you pay for solar can matter as much as which panels you buy. The same system can return large savings or barely break even depending on the deal. Here are the three routes and the questions that separate good offers from bad ones.

Cash purchase

How it works: you pay the installer up front and own the system outright.

  • Pros: the highest lifetime savings, any owner incentives are yours, and no interest.
  • Cons: a large upfront outlay, and the opportunity cost of money that could earn a return elsewhere.

Best for: homeowners with savings who plan to stay five years or more.

Solar loan

How it works: a lender pays the installer; you repay over 10 to 25 years and own the system from day one.

  • Pros: little or no money down while you still own the system.
  • Cons: interest, and sometimes hidden "dealer fees" built into the price to advertise a low rate.

Ask for the cash price and the loan price side by side. If the financed price is higher, that difference is a fee. A home-equity loan or credit-union loan at a fair rate can beat installer financing.

Lease or power purchase agreement (PPA)

How it works: a company owns panels on your roof. With a lease you pay a fixed monthly amount; with a PPA you pay per kWh the system produces, usually below the utility rate.

  • Pros: no upfront cost; the provider handles maintenance; in the US, the provider may still access commercial tax credits that homeowners lost.
  • Cons: smaller savings, escalators that raise payments each year, and transfer paperwork when you sell.

Five questions for any financing offer

  1. What is the cash price, and what is the total I will pay over the full term?
  2. Is there a dealer fee or origination fee, and how much?
  3. Does the payment rise each year, and by how much?
  4. What happens if I sell the house: transfer, buyout, or prepay?
  5. Who maintains and insures the system, and who pays for inverter replacement?

Selling a home with solar

Owned systems generally add value and are simple to sell. Loans may need paying off at closing, depending on whether they are secured on the property. Leases and PPAs require the buyer to qualify and agree to take over the contract, which some buyers resist. Read transfer clauses before signing.

Frequently asked

Is a $0-down offer really free?

No. It is financing. You pay through a loan, lease or PPA. It can still be a good deal, but compare the total cost over the contract.

Can I pay off a solar loan early?

Most solar loans allow prepayment without penalty, but check. Some loans assume you'll make a lump-sum payment early and raise the monthly payment if you don't.
