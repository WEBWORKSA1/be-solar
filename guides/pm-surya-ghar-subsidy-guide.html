PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana: rooftop solar subsidy guide for India

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

Subsidy amounts by system size, who qualifies, how to apply and how to estimate your savings.

India's PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is the central government's rooftop solar program for homes. It pays a fixed subsidy towards residential grid-connected systems, with the aim of helping households generate their own power and cut electricity bills.

Subsidy amounts

System sizeGeneral category statesSpecial category states and UTs*
1 kW₹30,000₹33,000
2 kW₹60,000₹66,000
3 kW and above₹78,000 (maximum)₹85,800 (maximum)

*Reported special-category rates apply to the North-Eastern states, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep. Housing societies and RWAs can receive support for common facilities at a per-kW rate up to a set capacity. Figures as reported in 2026 by industry press summarising ministry guidelines; confirm on the official portal before you apply.

The subsidy covers the first 2 kW at the full per-kW rate and the third kilowatt at a lower rate. Systems larger than 3 kW are allowed, but the extra capacity receives no subsidy.

Who qualifies

  • An Indian household with its own house and a roof suitable for solar.
  • A valid electricity connection in the applicant's name.
  • No previous central subsidy for rooftop solar on the same connection.
  • Panels and cells must meet domestic content rules, so vendors must use approved made-in-India equipment.

How to apply

  1. Register on the official PM Surya Ghar national portal with your state, electricity distribution company (DISCOM) and consumer number.
  2. Apply for rooftop solar and wait for technical feasibility approval from your DISCOM.
  3. Choose a registered vendor and have the system installed.
  4. Submit installation details and apply for a net meter.
  5. After commissioning and inspection, submit bank details; the subsidy is credited to your account.

Concessional loans linked to the scheme are available through public sector banks for systems up to a set size, often with simplified documentation.

What it saves

A 3 kW system in most of India produces roughly 3,600 to 4,500 kWh a year, around 300 to 375 units a month. At a typical residential tariff of ₹6–8 per unit, that's ₹22,000 to ₹36,000 of electricity a year. With installed costs commonly around ₹1.6–2 lakh for 3 kW and a ₹78,000 subsidy, payback is often four to six years.

Practical tips

  • Clean panels regularly in the dry season; dust can cut output noticeably in north and west India.
  • Ask the vendor for the module and inverter datasheets and a written service commitment of at least five years.
  • Check whether your state adds its own subsidy on top of the central amount.
  • Keep copies of every portal submission and invoice until the subsidy is credited.

Frequently asked

Is the electricity really free?

The scheme's name refers to free electricity because a well-sized system can cover around 300 units a month, which may eliminate the energy portion of many bills. Fixed charges may still apply.

Can tenants apply?

The applicant generally needs to hold the electricity connection and own or control the roof. Tenants should speak with their landlord.
