Solar panels in the UK: costs, 0% VAT and export payments

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

What a typical UK system costs, how the VAT relief and Smart Export Guarantee work, and how long payback takes.

Despite modest sunshine, solar is popular in the UK because electricity is expensive. A typical home system pays for itself in roughly nine to twelve years, faster if you use most of the power yourself.

What it costs

The Energy Saving Trust puts a typical 4.5 kWp system at around £7,600 installed. Most homes fit between 3.5 and 5 kWp. Adding a battery commonly adds several thousand pounds.

0% VAT until March 2027

Installed solar panels on homes, and batteries installed in residential settings, carry 0% VAT from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027, according to HMRC guidance. From 1 April 2027, the rate is due to return to 5% (the reduced rate), not the standard 20%. The relief applies to installation by a business, not to buying panels alone.

Smart Export Guarantee

Under the Smart Export Guarantee (SEG), larger electricity suppliers must offer a tariff for power you export. They set their own rates, which must be above zero. In 2026, headline rates range from a few pence per kWh to around 12p, with some supplier-linked deals paying more if you also buy their equipment or import tariff. To qualify, your installation and installer usually need to be MCS-certified, and you need a smart or export meter.

Because export rates are well below import prices, using solar power at home is worth more than exporting it. A battery or EV can raise self-use substantially.

Planning permission

Most domestic rooftop systems in England are permitted development and don't need planning permission, provided panels don't protrude too far from the roof. Listed buildings, conservation areas and flats have extra rules; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own regimes.

Other schemes

  • Government-funded energy-efficiency schemes and some local council grants can fund solar for eligible low-income households; eligibility rules change, so check GOV.UK and your council.
  • Scotland has offered interest-free loans for home energy upgrades through Home Energy Scotland; check current availability.

Payback example

A 4.5 kWp system in southern England produces around 4,000 kWh a year. If you use half at about 25p per kWh and export half at 12p, that's roughly £740 a year, a payback of about ten years on £7,600. Use the calculator with the UK selected to model your home.

Frequently asked

Do I need MCS certification?

Not for the VAT relief, but SEG tariffs generally require an MCS-certified installation. Most homeowners should choose an MCS installer anyway for consumer protection.

Are UK homes too far north for solar?

No. Scotland produces less than southern England, but a well-oriented system in Scotland still typically generates a worthwhile amount, and long summer days help.
