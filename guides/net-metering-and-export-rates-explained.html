Net metering and export rates, explained

By the Be.Solar editorial team. Updated October 21, 2026.

What happens to the solar power you don't use, why export rates are falling, and how that changes system size and batteries.

On a sunny afternoon, a typical home solar system makes more power than the house uses. The surplus flows to the grid through your meter. What your utility pays or credits for that power decides a large share of solar's value.

Classic net metering

Under full retail net metering, your meter effectively runs backwards. Each kWh exported earns a credit equal to the price you pay to import one. The grid acts like a free battery, and system size simply follows annual usage.

Net billing and export tariffs

Many places have moved to paying less for exports than for imports. California's 2023 switch to net billing, for example, values exports based on the utility's avoided costs, which are usually far below retail rates. In the UK, suppliers set their own Smart Export Guarantee rates, typically a fraction of import prices. Australian feed-in tariffs have fallen to a few cents per kWh in many states.

Why self-consumption now matters

When exports are worth less, every kWh you use yourself is worth more than one you export. Practical ways to use more of your own solar:

  • Run dishwashers, laundry and pool pumps in the middle of the day with timers.
  • Charge an EV at home around midday, or with a solar-aware charger.
  • Heat water with a heat pump water heater or a solar diverter.
  • Pre-cool or pre-heat the home with a heat pump in the afternoon.
  • Add a battery to shift evening use, especially under time-of-use tariffs.

Time-of-use rates

Many utilities charge more in the late afternoon and evening. West-facing panels produce more in that window and can be worth more per kWh than south-facing ones, even though they produce less in total. A battery charged at noon and drained at 6 pm captures the gap.

How export rates change sizing

Under full net metering, cover 100% of annual usage. Under low export rates, size for daytime use, often 60 to 80% of the bill, unless a battery or EV will absorb the surplus. Our savings calculator counts only self-used energy by default, which is conservative.

How to find your rules

  1. Search your utility's website for "net metering", "net billing", "export tariff" or "feed-in tariff".
  2. Note the export rate, any monthly fixed charges for solar customers, and how credits roll over.
  3. Ask installers to model savings under your exact tariff, not a generic one.
Rules can change after you install. Many jurisdictions protect existing customers for a set period. Ask whether your terms are guaranteed, and for how long.

Frequently asked

Will my utility pay me cash for extra solar?

Usually you receive bill credits. Some programs pay out surplus credits once a year, often at a low rate. UK SEG payments are made by your export supplier.

Do I need a new meter?

Usually, yes: a bidirectional or smart meter that records imports and exports separately. Your utility typically installs it after the system passes inspection.
